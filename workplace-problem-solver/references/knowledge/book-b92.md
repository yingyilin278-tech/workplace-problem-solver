# B92 — Originals

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Adam Grant, organizational psychologist and professor at Wharton
- Source type: Research-based book on idea championing and dissent
- Coverage: C4, C6, C8, C12

Official sources:

- https://adamgrant.net/book/originals/
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/247413/originals-by-adam-grant/9780143128854/

## Required user information

- The user's current idea, its stage, and the audience.
- The user's authority and the organization's tolerance for dissent.
- The cost of championing the idea versus waiting.
- The user's track record of follow-through.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Employees championing new ideas.
- Managers evaluating proposals.
- Founders and intrapreneurs.

Power asymmetry:

Dissent is costlier for lower-power employees. Encourage structural protections (anonymous channels, designated critics) before relying on individual courage.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Whistleblower protection and labor law are jurisdiction-specific. Encourage users to know the local rules before speaking up.

## Core claims

- Originals are not the risk-takers of myth; many are thoughtful, fastidious, and good at managing doubt.
- Idea championing requires balancing passion with strategic delay.
- Dissent improves group decisions; "pre-mortems" catch failures before they happen.
- Procrastination, used deliberately, can improve the quality of creative work.

## Decision rules

- If the user is in a high-risk environment, vet the idea with trusted peers before championing.
- If the user wants to speak up about a risk, use a pre-mortem format to invite the team to surface concerns.
- If the user is afraid of being the only dissenter, build a coalition before raising the issue.
- If the user is a manager, protect and reward productive dissent.

## Action steps

1. Run a pre-mortem: assume the idea has failed; identify why.
2. Identify the smallest evidence that would change the team's view.
3. Choose the right moment and venue to raise the idea.
4. Build a small coalition of supporters before the formal pitch.
5. Track idea outcomes to learn what works in this organization.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: team; channel: meeting; timing: when evaluating a plan. Wording: "假设这个项目一年后失败了，最可能的原因是什么？我们可以提前做什么？"
- Audience: manager; channel: written note; timing: when sharing a new idea. Wording: "这个想法我还在测试，但我想提前 30 秒听一下你的反应。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not treat delay as endorsement; the user must still commit when the moment comes.
- Do not use the framework to justify endless analysis.
- Do not use dissent language to mask personal grievances.

## Contraindications

- Cases where the user's idea conflicts with safety, legal, or compliance requirements.
- Workplaces with documented retaliation against dissenters.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is an organizational psychologist; framework is research-based.
- Rule conflicts: ['Championing is riskier in high-hierarchy or low-psychological-safety environments.', 'Strategic delay can become avoidance.']
- Copyright scope: Summarize concepts. Do not reproduce extended passages.
