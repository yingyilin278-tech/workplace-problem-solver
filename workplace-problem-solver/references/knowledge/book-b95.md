## Required user information

- The type of judgment being made: hiring, evaluation, pricing, performance.
- The number of decision makers and the structure of the process.
- The cost of variability in the decision.
- Available data, rubrics, and feedback loops.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Hiring managers, performance evaluators, and pricing committees.
- Auditors, judges, and reviewers.
- HR and risk partners.

Power asymmetry:

Decisions with high variability disproportionately affect lower-power individuals (candidates, employees, defendants). Use noise reduction to protect them.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Decision hygiene is universally applicable; specific rubrics must respect local law, including anti-discrimination requirements.

## Core claims

- Human judgment is unreliable: the same person can make different decisions on the same case at different times.
- Bias is not the only source of error; noise is a separate, large, and reducible source.
- Decision hygiene—structured rubrics, independent judgments, aggregation—reduces both bias and noise.
- "Broken leg" cues: any irrelevant context that influences judgment is noise to be controlled.

## Decision rules

- If the decision is judgment-based, define a structured rubric before reviewing the case.
- If multiple judges will evaluate, collect independent judgments before any discussion.
- If the decision has high stakes, aggregate multiple judgments rather than relying on a single voice.
- If the user wants to argue against a decision, identify the specific rubric score and the evidence, not impressions.

## Action steps

1. Define a clear rubric with weighted dimensions before evaluating.
2. Have each evaluator score independently, then compare.
3. Discuss the differences only after independent scores are recorded.
4. Aggregate judgments rather than relying on a single assessor.
5. Audit decision outcomes to detect patterns of bias or noise.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: hiring team; channel: meeting; timing: before reviewing candidates. Wording: "我们先各自独立打分，再对比；不讨论候选人个人。"
- Audience: manager; channel: written note; timing: when defending an evaluation. Wording: "这次评估的三个维度是 [A/B/C]，[A] 分数是 [x]，[B] 是 [y]，[C] 是 [z]；请关注具体证据。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not assume more discussion reduces noise; it often increases it.
- Do not use rubrics to evade accountability for hard calls.
- Do not aggregate across judges who used different rubrics.

## Contraindications

- Decisions that require holistic professional judgment (e.g., medical diagnosis) without supporting rubrics.
- Cases where the rubric itself encodes bias.
