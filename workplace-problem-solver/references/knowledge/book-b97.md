# B97 — The Innovator's Dilemma

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Clayton Christensen, Harvard Business School professor and author
- Source type: Research-based book on disruptive innovation
- Coverage: C7, C8, C12

## Required user information

- The user's role: incumbent leader, new entrant, or investor.
- The trajectory of the technology or product in question.
- The organization's current customers, cost structure, and processes.
- The time horizon of decision makers.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Senior executives and strategists.
- Product and innovation leaders.
- Investors and board members.

Power asymmetry:

Incumbents can defer hard innovation decisions because of short-term performance pressure; the framework helps surface that conflict.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The framework is industry-agnostic; regulatory and labor implications are jurisdiction-specific.

## Core claims

- Successful incumbents often fail to adopt disruptive technologies because their existing customers, processes, and economics reward sustaining innovation.
- Disruptive innovations start at the low end or in new markets and improve over time.
- Spinning off a separate organization is often required to nurture a disruptive business.
- Listening to current customers is necessary but insufficient for long-term survival.

## Decision rules

- If the new technology does not meet current customer needs, evaluate whether a new customer segment does.
- If the existing organization cannot pursue the new opportunity without cannibalization, spin it off or buy rather than build.
- If the time horizon of the business case is longer than the management horizon, change the structure.
- If the user is in an emerging segment, prioritize speed over efficiency.

## Action steps

1. Identify the technology trajectory and the under-served segments.
2. Separate the sustaining and disruptive roadmaps.
3. Set up a structure (team, spin-off, acquisition) that protects the disruptive bet.
4. Define milestones that confirm or refute the trajectory hypothesis.
5. Review the strategy annually against the original assumptions.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: leadership team; channel: meeting; timing: when reviewing strategy. Wording: "如果今天我们最大的客户群五年后不再是主要增长来源，我们现在应该投资什么？"
- Audience: product team; channel: written note; timing: at start of a new project. Wording: "这个新方向可能不会立刻服务今天的大客户，但会服务下一个阶段的客户。先界定最小可行测试。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use the framework to justify ignoring profitable core customers.
- Do not assume every new technology is disruptive; some are sustaining.
- Do not launch spin-offs without clear strategic rationale.

## Contraindications

- Industries with strict safety or licensing constraints that prevent a separate structure.
- Cases where cannibalization is not a real risk and the new bet can be built inside.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author was a senior academic; framework is widely cited but not all industries follow the pattern.
- Rule conflicts: ['Some "disruptive" labels are marketing, not strategy.', 'Spinning off can create coordination problems.']
