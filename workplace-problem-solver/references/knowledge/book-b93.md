# B93 — Option B

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Sheryl Sandberg and Adam Grant, organizational leaders writing on resilience after loss
- Source type: Research-based book with personal narrative
- Coverage: C5, C10, C11

Official sources:

- https://optionbbook.com/
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/238403/option-b-by-sheryl-sandberg-adam-grant/9781524752647/

## Required user information

- The nature of the loss and the user's current coping stage.
- The user's support network and the willingness to ask for help.
- The user's work demands and accommodations available.
- Any mental-health symptoms that require professional care.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Individuals coping with personal or professional loss.
- Managers and HR partners supporting bereaved team members.
- Coaches and community leaders.

Power asymmetry:

Resilience framing should not be used to rush someone through grief or to avoid providing accommodations.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Bereavement, leave, and accommodation rules are jurisdiction-specific. Confirm the user's local entitlements.

## Core claims

- Resilience is built by leaning into pain, not by suppressing it.
- The "three P's"—personalization, pervasiveness, permanence—shape how setbacks distort thinking.
- Post-traumatic growth is a documented phenomenon, not a platitude.
- Small acts of help from others are decisive in recovery.

## Decision rules

- If the user is in acute grief, prioritize safety and support over performance.
- If the user is catastrophizing, gently test the pervasiveness and permanence of the belief.
- If the user wants to be alone, schedule one short check-in rather than disappear.
- If the user is a manager of a bereaved person, model concrete help and respect boundaries.

## Action steps

1. Name what was lost and what cannot be replaced.
2. Identify the small supports that would lighten the next week.
3. Schedule one "joy" moment daily, even if brief.
4. Ask a trusted friend or colleague to be the daily check-in.
5. Track three things the user is grateful for, weekly.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: self; channel: private note; timing: daily. Wording: "今天我做了一件小的、能完成的事。它是 [具体行为]。"
- Audience: manager; channel: written message; timing: at the start of accommodation. Wording: "我希望你不需要立刻回复工作邮件。如果有紧急的事，我会直接发短信给你。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use resilience language to push the user back to work prematurely.
- Do not assume the user wants to talk about the loss; ask first.
- Do not equate grief with depression or require professional help unless indicated.

## Contraindications

- Active suicidal ideation or self-harm risk—escalate to emergency services.
- Complicated grief or trauma responses requiring clinical support.
- Workplaces that pressure premature return.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Authors are public figures with personal narrative; the book mixes memoir and research.
- Rule conflicts: ['Resilience framing should not bypass professional care.', 'Some losses require longer recovery than organizations allow.']
- Copyright scope: Summarize the three P's and small-help concept. Do not reproduce extended passages.
