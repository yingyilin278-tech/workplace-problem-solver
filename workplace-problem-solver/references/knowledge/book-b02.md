## Required user information

- The negotiable issues, each party's stated positions, and the interests or constraints beneath them.
- Who has authority to commit, change scope, allocate resources, or approve an exception.
- The user's alternatives if no agreement is reached and the practical cost of each alternative.
- Objective criteria such as policy, market benchmarks, prior agreements, performance data, or professional standards.
- Deadlines, dependencies, power asymmetry, confidentiality, and any legal or irreversible decision point.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Employees negotiating workload, role scope, compensation, performance criteria, or an exit.
- Managers allocating resources and resolving competing priorities.
- Peers and cross-functional teams negotiating ownership, inputs, deadlines, and standards.
- HR and executives designing or facilitating agreements.

Power asymmetry:

Interest-based negotiation is useful under hierarchy, but the stronger party may refuse to negotiate or control the criteria and alternatives. The lower-power party should improve lawful alternatives, document proposals, avoid bluffing, and distinguish a genuine choice from a compelled waiver.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The principles travel across settings, but acceptable bargaining behavior, authority, face, silence, and disclosure vary by culture. Objective criteria and BATNAs must be grounded in the relevant jurisdiction, contract, policy, and actual organizational practice.

## Core claims

- Separate the people from the problem so that relationship management and substantive analysis do not collapse into personal attack.
- Focus on interests rather than fixed positions to reveal constraints and possible trades.
- Generate multiple options before deciding, rather than treating the first visible compromise as the only solution.
- Use fair and independent criteria instead of a contest of will where possible.
- Evaluate any proposed agreement against the best alternative to a negotiated agreement rather than treating agreement itself as success.

## Decision rules

- If positions conflict, ask what need, risk, deadline, or constraint makes each position important before proposing a split.
- If a new task displaces an agreed task, present the capacity consequence and ask the person with authority to choose the priority.
- If parties dispute fairness, jointly identify independent standards and test whether they are relevant and consistently applied.
- If a proposed agreement is worse than the user's realistic best alternative, do not accept merely to preserve harmony unless a separate legal or safety duty changes the calculation.
- If the other party uses coercion, deception, or nonnegotiable authority, stop treating the matter as ordinary collaborative bargaining and consider formal safeguards.

## Action steps

1. List issues, interests, authority, constraints, deadlines, and relationship concerns.
2. Develop and reality-test the user's best alternative without threats or invented leverage.
3. Ask neutral questions to surface the other party's interests and decision criteria.
4. Generate at least two implementable packages that vary scope, time, resources, or ownership.
5. Compare packages against objective criteria and the user's alternative.
6. Confirm the agreement, owners, deliverables, dates, review points, and unresolved items in writing.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: manager; channel: email or work chat; timing: before accepting conflicting deadlines. Wording: 'I can take the new request. With current capacity, completing it today moves the Friday report to Monday. The workable alternatives are a first draft today with the report unchanged, or a final version today with the report rescheduled. Please confirm the priority.'
- Audience: peer or cross-functional owner; channel: meeting followed by notes; timing: when positions harden. Wording: 'Rather than debate which team should absorb the work, can we list the deadline, quality, and resource constraints on both sides and test two allocation options against the project target?'
- Audience: compensation decision maker; channel: scheduled meeting; timing: after preparing benchmarks. Wording: 'I would like to compare the role's current scope and results with the agreed level criteria and relevant market data, then discuss the adjustment or development plan those standards support.'

## Counterexamples

- The counterparty has no authority to change the result; negotiating with that person consumes time without producing a decision.
- The user's claimed alternative is not feasible; invoking it as leverage becomes a bluff and can damage credibility.
- The organization presents a biased internal rule as objective while applying it selectively.
- A safety, discrimination, wage, dismissal, or statutory deadline issue requires formal advice or escalation, not only option generation.
- An abusive counterpart uses interest questions to obtain information for retaliation without offering reciprocal problem solving.

## Contraindications

- Immediate threats, violence, medical crisis, or urgent safeguarding.
- Definitive legal advice about termination, severance, wages, discrimination, evidence, or waivers.
- A compelled signature or appeal deadline where negotiation could cause the user to lose procedural rights.
- Any instruction to conceal evidence, misrepresent alternatives, collude, or evade mandatory rules.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: The authors built careers in negotiation education, advising, mediation, and related organizations. Their framework favors joint problem solving, principled standards, and negotiated settlement, which may underweight unilateral enforcement, collective action, structural inequality, or strategic noncooperation.
- Rule conflicts: ['Labor law, contract terms, collective bargaining rules, and internal procedures may limit what can be traded.', 'Some distributive negotiations have little joint value to create; preparation, leverage, and reservation limits remain necessary.', "Powerful parties may reject independent criteria or define the user's alternative.", 'Relationship-preserving negotiation can conflict with prompt reporting duties or the need to create a clear record of misconduct.']
