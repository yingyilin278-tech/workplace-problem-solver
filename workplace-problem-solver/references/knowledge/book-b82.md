# B82 — The Fearless Organization (companion category)

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Practice and research syntheses extending Amy Edmondson's psychological safety framework into specific industries
- Source type: Industry-applied practitioner book
- Coverage: C2, C4, C10, C12

Official sources:

## Required user information

- The team or unit type (clinical, engineering, sales, customer service, etc.).
- Current psychological safety signals (silence on bad news, hidden errors, low engagement).
- Manager behaviors that may be undermining candor.
- Whether the team faces time pressure, regulation, or hierarchical constraints.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Frontline managers, especially in regulated or high-pressure industries.
- HR partners designing culture interventions.
- Senior leaders diagnosing organizational silence.

Power asymmetry:

Psychological safety is not a substitute for procedural justice. Use the model alongside formal safeguards for whistleblowing, harassment, and patient or customer safety.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Industry-specific regulations (healthcare, finance, aviation) impose mandatory reporting and candor duties that the framework should not override.

## Core claims

- Psychological safety is a team-level property, not a personality trait.
- Specific leader behaviors—inviting input, responding non-defensively, framing failure as learning—shift team candor.
- Industry context changes which leader behaviors are most effective.
- Silence about errors, risks, or bad news is an early warning of preventable harm.

## Decision rules

- If a team hides errors, treat the manager's response to the first disclosed error as the highest-leverage intervention.
- If a high-stakes environment demands a rapid, top-down decision, name the situation explicitly and invite dissent in a defined window.
- If psychological safety work is being used to suppress hard performance management, redesign the model.
- If safety-sensitive errors recur, add structural safeguards (checklists, escalation rules) before further culture work.

## Action steps

1. Identify the team's three highest-leverage candor gaps.
2. Set one team norm per quarter that addresses one gap.
3. Practice "fallibility statements" and "ask don't tell" in a team meeting.
4. Track small disclosures and how the leader responds.
5. Review results with the team and adjust the next norm.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: team; channel: meeting opening; timing: at the start of a project. Wording: "我可能会漏掉一些信息，请把你们看到的风险直接说出来，我会跟进。"
- Audience: team; channel: after a near-miss; timing: weekly review. Wording: "这个错误教会我们什么？我们下一步要改什么？"

## Counterexamples

- Do not treat psychological safety as a substitute for accountability.
- Do not use it to delay a needed corrective action.
- Do not assume candor will rise without changes to leader behavior.

## Contraindications

- Active safety incidents requiring formal investigation.
- Cases requiring confidentiality, such as ongoing grievance or legal processes.
- Situations where disclosure itself could cause legal liability without whistleblower protection.

## Conflicts and limitations
