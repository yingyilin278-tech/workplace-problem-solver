## Required user information

- The decision under consideration, its stakes, and its reversibility.
- The user's emotional state, fatigue, and time pressure.
- Available data, expertise, and disagreement among advisors.
- The presence of incentives that could distort judgment.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Decision makers at any level facing consequential choices.
- Analysts, advisors, and reviewers.
- Managers structuring group decisions.

Power asymmetry:

Bias awareness is not a substitute for diverse input; include lower-power voices to test assumptions.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Biases operate across contexts, but the specific interventions must fit local law, norms, and constraints.

## Core claims

- Human cognition runs two systems: fast (intuition) and slow (deliberation).
- Heuristics produce systematic biases in prediction, evaluation, and risk assessment.
- Overconfidence is a major source of decision failure.
- Replacing human judgment with simple algorithms often improves accuracy, even when the algorithm is imperfect.

## Decision rules

- If the decision is reversible, prefer fast action and learn.
- If the decision is irreversible, slow down and use a checklist.
- If the user is tired, angry, or rushed, defer major decisions.
- If disagreement exists, seek out the dissenters and steelman their view.

## Action steps

1. Identify the decision's reversibility and time pressure.
2. List the assumptions being made and the evidence for each.
3. Run a pre-mortem: assume failure, identify causes.
4. Replace the lowest-quality intuitions with a simple rule or check.
5. Schedule a review point to learn from the outcome.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: team; channel: meeting; timing: when making a major decision. Wording: "这个决定如果错了，最坏的结果是什么？我们怎么能在做出前看到那个信号？"
- Audience: self; channel: private note; timing: at decision time. Wording: "我现在是直觉在说话还是证据在说话？"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use bias awareness to avoid all decisions.
- Do not assume algorithms or checklists are always superior; they too have limits.
- Do not use the framework to dismiss minority views.

## Contraindications

- High-stakes, life-critical decisions requiring expert advice.
- Cases where the user is in acute distress and cannot engage slow thinking.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is a senior researcher; framework is widely used but the operational rules are simplified.
- Rule conflicts: ['Some biases are not correctable by awareness alone.', 'Group dynamics can re-create bias even with checklists.']
