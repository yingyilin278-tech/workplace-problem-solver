## Required user information

- The decision under consideration, the alternatives considered, and the desired outcome.
- The user's confidence in each piece of evidence.
- The reversibility of the decision and the cost of being wrong.
- The user's emotional state and the risk of motivated reasoning.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Decision makers facing uncertainty.
- Coaches and advisors.
- Teams practicing after-action reviews.

Power asymmetry:

Encourage the user to test their own reasoning; lower-power team members often have the most accurate read on risk and should be invited in.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The framework is broadly applicable; specific decisions remain subject to local law and policy.

## Core claims

- Most decisions are bets, not certainties; treat them as such.
- A good decision can have a bad outcome, and vice versa; we should evaluate decision quality, not just outcomes.
- "Resulting"—judging a decision by its outcome rather than its process—leads to poor learning.
- Explicit confidence levels and a "buddy" reviewer reduce motivated reasoning.

## Decision rules

- If the user is being judged by the outcome alone, ask what they would have known at decision time.
- If the user is overconfident, force a confidence level out of 100 and seek disconfirming evidence.
- If the user is avoiding accountability, separate the quality of the decision from the luck of the outcome.
- If the team is repeating the same mistake, run a structured after-action review.

## Action steps

1. State the decision, the alternatives, and the predicted outcomes.
2. Assign a confidence level out of 100 for each predicted outcome.
3. List the evidence that would change the prediction.
4. Schedule a review point to compare predicted vs. actual outcomes.
5. Capture lessons and update decision patterns.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: team; channel: meeting; timing: after a setback. Wording: "这次结果不好，但当时在同样信息下做的决定是否可以不同？"
- Audience: self; channel: private note; timing: when making a bet. Wording: "我对这个判断的信心是 [0-100]；如果信心低于 [x]，我需要更多证据。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use the framework to avoid accountability for negligence.
- Do not treat "good process, bad outcome" as a license to repeat risky decisions.
- Do not use confidence levels as performance scores for others.

## Contraindications

- Decisions that require accountability for safety, ethics, or law regardless of process.
- Cases where the user is in acute distress and cannot reflect.
