## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- MBA, EMBA, or advanced business student
- HR or labor-relations professional in mainland China
- Manager or employer representative
- Union or employee representative
- Employee using the book to understand labor-relations institutions and likely employer processes
- Policy, management, or labor-relations researcher

Power asymmetry:

The book recognizes employers, workers, unions, and government as distinct labor-relations actors, but workplace application still occurs under unequal control of records, assignments, pay, discipline, and termination. Employee-facing use should preserve voice, representation, safe response time, and lawful records. Manager-facing use should not convert a general management strategy into proof that a contested action is fair or lawful.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The text is centered on mainland China's labor-relations system and 2025 publication context, while also introducing international labor rules and selected foreign models. National law, judicial interpretations, local adjudication practice, collective institutions, and rules for newer forms of work can change. It is not transferable to Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan, or another country without separate legal and institutional research.

## Core claims

- Publisher-scope claim: enterprise labor relations should be understood through the interaction of employers, workers, unions, and government rather than reduced to an internal HR function.
- Publisher-scope claim: the book links labor-relations theory with mainland China practices in labor standards, employment contracts, collective consultation, workplace rules, democratic participation, dispute handling, and newer forms of work.
- Author-scope claim: coordinated labor relations require both legal compliance and institutions through which interests can be expressed, negotiated, and adjusted.
- Author-scope claim: real cases can help learners connect abstract theories and statutory mechanisms with enterprise decisions.
- Boundary claim: a textbook discussion or historical case does not establish the current legal result in a live employment dispute.

## Decision rules

- If a workplace issue affects pay, hours, duties, rules, contract status, discipline, or termination, then identify the relevant actor, interest, legal basis, and coordination mechanism before selecting an HR response.
- If an employer proposes a rule directly affecting workers, then verify current legal requirements for content, consultation, communication, implementation, and review before relying on the rule.
- If individual discussion cannot resolve a shared workforce issue, then assess whether employee representatives, a union, collective consultation, or a tripartite mechanism is available, unless urgent safety action is required first.
- If a case involves a newer work arrangement, then determine the worker's actual legal status and current authoritative rules rather than assuming that a conventional employment model applies.
- If a textbook proposition conflicts with current authoritative law or qualified local advice, then the current authoritative source takes priority.

## Action steps

1. Identify the parties, roles, power differences, jurisdiction, worker status, event date, and decision deadline.
2. Build a dated factual chronology and collect relevant records lawfully.
3. Classify the issue as an individual right, collective interest, management-rule, labor-standard, contract, participation, or dispute problem.
4. Map each party's stated interests, legal duties, available evidence, and authority to decide.
5. Use the book's framework and cases to create an issue checklist, not a final legal conclusion.
6. Verify current statutes, judicial interpretations, local rules, and authoritative administrative or court guidance.
7. Choose the least irreversible lawful coordination step, document the agreed process, and escalate high-stakes disputes to qualified local support.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Employee or representative to HR, written request before a material change: "Please provide the proposed change, its factual and legal basis, the consultation process, the effective date, and the channel through which employees may submit comments or objections."
- HR to management, internal review before implementing a rule: "Please separate the business objective from the legal and labor-relations process. We need to confirm content legality, consultation, communication, consistent application, and the response route before implementation."
- Manager to employee or union representative, formal meeting: "The current issue concerns these facts and interests. I propose that we first confirm the record, then identify the applicable rule and the available individual or collective consultation mechanism before deciding next steps."

## Counterexamples

- A formally published rule may still fail if its content, consultation process, notice, or application is defective.
- A cooperative relationship narrative can conceal retaliation or unequal bargaining power when workers lack a safe channel to disagree.
- A collective mechanism may not resolve an urgent individual deadline or protect a worker without timely evidence preservation.
- An official historical case may no longer reflect later legislation, judicial interpretation, or local practice.
- Practices developed for standard employees may not fit dispatched, platform, foreign, part-time, or other specially regulated workers.

## Contraindications

- Definitive legal advice without current national and local verification
- Use outside mainland China without jurisdiction-specific research
- Advice to fabricate, destroy, secretly obtain, or unlawfully disclose evidence
- Pressure to sign a resignation, waiver, settlement, admission, or other irreversible document without informed review
- Use of harmonious-relations language to suppress lawful employee voice, representation, reporting, or remedies

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Yuhua Xie writes from an academic labor-relations and corporate-social-responsibility perspective, while Chunlei Liu adds enterprise HR, state-owned-enterprise restructuring, and management-system experience. The combination broadens the text but may still privilege formal institutions and managerial coordination, and the authors' mainland China policy context may underrepresent informal labor, adversarial worker advocacy, and jurisdictions with different union structures.
- Rule conflicts: ['A cooperative or pluralist labor-relations framework can conflict with strongly managerial, adversarial, or worker-mobilization approaches.', 'Enterprise efficiency can conflict with worker protection, collective voice, procedural justice, and distributive fairness.', 'General national principles can conflict with local arbitration and court practice.', 'International labor concepts can conflict with the specific legal status and institutional role of unions in mainland China.', 'A 2025 textbook can be superseded by later statutes, judicial interpretations, policies, or controlling decisions.']
- Copyright scope: Safe use includes translated bibliographic facts, high-level chapter topics, issue spotting, and independently written paraphrases. Do not reproduce the sample chapter, detailed cases, tables, figures, teaching resources, or substantial portions of the book. Retrieve current statutory and official case text from authoritative sources.
