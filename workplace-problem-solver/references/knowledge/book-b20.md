## Required user information

- The audience and the decision, answer, or action needed
- The audience's starting knowledge and the question likely to be in their mind
- The main conclusion and the strongest supporting ideas
- Whether the support is deductive, inductive, chronological, structural, or priority-based
- The evidence, uncertainty, counterevidence, and details that belong in appendices

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Employee
- Manager
- Consultant
- Executive
- Project lead
- Analyst
- HR or performance reviewer

Power asymmetry:

A conclusion-first structure can help a lower-power employee reduce cognitive load for a busy decision-maker, but it does not eliminate retaliation or disagreement risk. When challenging authority, lead with a bounded work conclusion, evidence, risk, and request while preserving the full record. Do not compress away facts needed for due process.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The method is a general business-communication framework rather than jurisdiction-specific advice. Conclusion-first communication may need adaptation in high-context, face-sensitive, academic, judicial, investigative, or public-administration settings where background, protocol, or evidentiary sequence carries special weight.

## Core claims

- Official-author claim: readers grasp complex thinking more easily when related ideas are grouped and summarized upward into a pyramid under one governing point.
- Official-author claim: clear communication depends on logical relationships both vertically between a point and its support and horizontally among ideas at the same level.
- Official-author claim: the Situation-Complication-Question framework identifies the question in the audience's mind and prepares the governing answer.
- Official-author claim: pyramid building is a thinking process as well as a writing format; it helps discover and test the message before drafting.
- Boundary claim: a clean structure improves comprehension but cannot make weak, incomplete, or misleading evidence sound.

## Decision rules

- If the audience must decide or act, then lead with the answer and group only the support required for that decision, unless law, protocol, or safety requires a different sequence.
- If supporting points overlap or mix abstraction levels, then regroup them until each set answers one question and is mutually coherent.
- If a document begins with extensive background, then use situation, complication, and question to determine the minimum context needed before the answer.
- If a list is merely a collection of topics, then identify the governing insight and an explicit ordering logic before presenting it.
- If uncertainty or contrary evidence could change the decision, then surface it near the relevant claim rather than hiding it in an appendix.

## Action steps

1. Write the audience's decision question in one sentence.
2. Draft a one-sentence answer that can stand at the top of the communication.
3. List the few major reasons, steps, or components that directly support the answer.
4. Choose one logical order for each group, such as time, structure, rank, or deduction.
5. Test vertical logic by asking how and why, and test horizontal logic by checking that grouped ideas are comparable and non-overlapping.
6. Create a short situation-complication-question opening only when context is needed.
7. Move evidence and detail into lower levels or appendices, while keeping material risks and uncertainty visible.
8. Revise headings so a reader can understand the argument from the headings alone.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Executive update, email before a decision: "Recommendation: delay the launch by one week. Three facts drive this recommendation: the security test is incomplete, the payment rollback is unverified, and customer support coverage is not staffed. Please approve the revised date by 4 p.m."
- Cross-functional meeting opening: "The decision today is whether to keep the date or reduce scope. My recommendation is to reduce scope because it protects the critical user path, requires no new headcount, and keeps the regulatory review intact."
- Performance clarification, written follow-up: "Conclusion: the current rating cannot yet be reconciled with the stated targets. The three gaps are the target version used, the treatment of the completed deliverable, and the absence of evidence for the quality deduction. Please confirm these three points before the review is finalized."

## Counterexamples

- A conclusion-first message can look predetermined when the purpose is joint discovery, listening, mediation, or psychological support.
- Strictly grouped categories can erase legitimate overlap in complex social or organizational systems.
- A concise executive summary can conceal uncertainty, minority views, or evidence that changes the decision.
- Situation-complication-question can become formulaic when no real complication or audience question exists.
- Investigations, legal filings, scientific reports, and regulated notices may require prescribed structures.

## Contraindications

- Use of structure to conceal evidence, manufacture certainty, or manipulate a decision
- Immediate safety, medical, or legal situations where mandated protocols control communication
- Formal investigations or disputes where compressing the record could remove necessary chronology or context
- Emotionally sensitive conversations where listening and acknowledgement must precede a recommendation

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Minto sells books, courses, and training based on the proprietary framework. Her consulting-oriented lens favors executive clarity, hierarchy, and decision efficiency, which can underweight participatory sense-making, emotional processing, nonlinear discovery, and the political effects of deciding which facts sit at the top.
- Rule conflicts: ['Conclusion-first structure can conflict with motivational interviewing, mediation, design discovery, or cultures that expect context before assertion.', 'Compression can conflict with legal due process, scientific transparency, audit trails, and evidence-preservation needs.', 'Mutually exclusive grouping can conflict with intersectional or systems analysis where causes legitimately overlap.', 'Audience-centered framing can become manipulation if it filters out material contrary evidence.']
- Copyright scope: Safe use includes bibliographic metadata, the general pyramid concept, the name of the Situation-Complication-Question framework, and original examples and paraphrases. Do not reproduce the textbook's chapters, diagrams, exercises, appendices, or proprietary course materials.
