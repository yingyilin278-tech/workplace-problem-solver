## Required user information

- The current workload, deadlines, and which commitments are user-chosen versus externally imposed.
- The user's health, sleep, family responsibilities, and risk of acute burnout.
- The user's beliefs about being indispensable, fear of disappointing others, or fear of being judged for refusing work.
- The reversibility of the career decision and the cost of a bounded experiment.
- Any legal, safety, discrimination, or retaliation context that would change whether "acceptance of limits" is even a safe frame.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Knowledge workers facing chronic overload.
- Mid-career professionals reconsidering priorities and scope.
- People considering a career change or a slowdown.
- Managers helping teams negotiate sustainable workload.

Power asymmetry:

This frame assumes the user can refuse, delegate, or reduce work. In a low-control, high-surveillance, or legally exposed environment, the advice is to clarify rights, document constraints, and seek internal or external support rather than absorb additional commitments silently.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The frame is broadly transferable. It does not establish legal rights, define overtime, address union contracts, or override safety and discrimination obligations.

## Core claims

- Human time and attention are finite; a workable career strategy begins by accepting limits rather than trying to control every demand.
- Productivity systems can become avoidance when they help a person feel in control without requiring a difficult priority decision.
- Choosing one priority necessarily means leaving other valuable possibilities undone.
- A sustainable response to overload is to make trade-offs explicit, reduce commitments, and tolerate the discomfort of not finishing everything.

## Decision rules

- If the workload exceeds available time, do not add another efficiency trick first; identify what must be abandoned, delayed, or renegotiated.
- If a task is repeatedly postponed, ask whether it is genuinely important or merely attractive in theory.
- If a career decision is reversible and the downside is limited, run a bounded experiment instead of waiting for certainty.
- If exhaustion is caused by unsafe conditions, discrimination, retaliation, or unlawful overwork, treat it as an organizational or legal-risk problem, not merely a mindset problem.

## Action steps

1. List all active commitments and mark each as essential, negotiable, deferrable, or removable.
2. Choose one primary outcome for the current period and define what will not receive priority.
3. Tell the relevant person what trade-off is required and ask them to confirm the order.
4. Set a stopping point for the workday or project; record unfinished items for the next decision cycle.
5. Review after one or two weeks: keep, reduce, delegate, renegotiate, or exit the commitment.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: manager; channel: written message after the team meeting; timing: same day. Wording: "目前我手上有 A、B、C 三项任务，按现有时间可以先保证 A；如果今天还要完成 D，需要把 B 或 C 顺延到明天。请确认优先级。"
- Audience: self or coach; channel: private note; timing: weekly review. Wording: "这件事我愿意试行两周。两周后我们根据结果决定继续、调整还是停止，不先把它承诺成长期方案。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use acceptance of limits to excuse chronic understaffing, harassment, wage violations, or retaliation.
- Do not tell a person facing acute mental-health symptoms that better prioritization alone will solve the problem.
- In a low-control role, "just say no" may create retaliation risk; use written clarification, evidence preservation, and escalation channels.

## Contraindications

- Acute safety, harassment, or mental-health crisis.
- Legal deadlines, contract obligations, or regulatory reporting windows.
- Work that affects others' health, safety, or fundamental rights and cannot be delayed.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Trade-book author framing; popular synthesis, not clinical or organizational research.
- Rule conflicts: ['Many workplaces use "choose your priorities" framing to shift risk to employees without changing workload.', 'Acceptance of limits can be misused to justify exploitative conditions.']
