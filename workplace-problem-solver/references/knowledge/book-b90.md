# B90 — Together

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Vivek Murthy, former U.S. Surgeon General, author writing on loneliness and connection
- Source type: Research-based book on social connection and health
- Coverage: C5, C8, C10, C12

Official sources:

- https://www.harpersanfrancisco.com/titles/vivek-h-murthy/together/9780062913296/
- https://www.vivekmurthy.com/book

## Required user information

- The user's current social connections and the quality of each.
- The user's work and life demands that affect time for relationships.
- Symptoms of loneliness, isolation, or disconnection.
- Available community, family, or workplace support.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Individuals seeking to rebuild connection.
- Managers and HR partners shaping team rituals.
- Community organizers and educators.

Power asymmetry:

Connection-building can be co-opted for surveillance or exclusion. Use the framework to invite voluntary participation.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The book's evidence base is primarily U.S. and Anglophone; adapt to local community structures and cultural norms.

## Core claims

- Loneliness is a public health issue with measurable physical and cognitive effects.
- Connection is built through small, consistent acts, not grand gestures.
- Workplaces can either create or relieve loneliness depending on rituals and design.
- Service to others is a reliable path back to connection.

## Decision rules

- If the user is chronically isolated, treat the smallest weekly contact as a baseline.
- If the workplace is the source of loneliness, identify the rituals that crowd out connection.
- If the user is grieving or in transition, allow time for reconnection rather than performance.
- If the user wants to deepen existing relationships, propose a recurring, low-pressure activity.

## Action steps

1. Audit the user's relationships across work, family, friends, and community.
2. Schedule one recurring weekly touchpoint with someone who matters.
3. Join or form a small group with a shared interest or service focus.
4. Replace some screen time with in-person or voice contact.
5. Review the user's connection score quarterly.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: self; channel: private note; timing: weekly. Wording: "本周我和谁有过一次有意义的对话？下周我打算联系谁？"
- Audience: team; channel: meeting; timing: when planning rituals. Wording: "除了工作目标，我们保留每月一次的午餐，让团队有时间互相了解。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use the framework to pressure disclosure in unsafe contexts.
- Do not assume that joining a group alone resolves loneliness.
- Do not romanticize busy "community" involvement that lacks depth.

## Contraindications

- Active grief or trauma requiring professional support.
- Environments where social connection is being used to manipulate.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is a public-health leader; framework is policy-informed.
- Rule conflicts: ['Some users face structural isolation (immigration, caregiving) that requires more than individual action.', 'Workplace connection cannot compensate for unsafe management.']
- Copyright scope: Summarize concepts. Do not reproduce extended passages.
