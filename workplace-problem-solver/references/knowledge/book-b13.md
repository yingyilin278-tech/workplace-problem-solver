## Required user information

- The roles and power relationship between feedback giver and receiver
- The specific behavior, context, observable result, and desired next step
- Whether trust and a two-way feedback channel already exist
- The recipient's response and any identity, cultural, disability, or language factors affecting delivery
- The organization's performance process, documentation requirements, and retaliation risk

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Manager
- Team lead
- Executive
- Employee giving peer feedback
- Employee giving upward feedback where conditions are safe
- HR or people leader designing feedback routines

Power asymmetry:

The framework places special responsibility on managers to solicit criticism before giving it, reward candor, and monitor how feedback lands. Upward candor is riskier than downward candor; employees should not be told that equal directness creates equal safety. Formal safeguards, private channels, and anti-retaliation practice may be necessary.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The framework emerged from United States technology and executive settings. Expectations about direct criticism, public praise, privacy, hierarchy, saving face, and performance documentation vary widely. Adapt the channel and language to local culture and law without turning ambiguity into hidden performance standards.

## Core claims

- Author-framework claim: effective guidance combines caring personally with challenging directly.
- Author-framework claim: care without sufficient challenge becomes ruinous empathy, while challenge without sufficient care becomes obnoxious aggression; low levels of both produce manipulative insincerity.
- Author-framework claim: praise should be specific and sincere, and criticism should be kind, clear, behavior-focused, and actionable.
- Author-framework claim: the feedback order of operations is to get feedback, give feedback, gauge how it lands, and encourage feedback between others.
- Author-framework claim: feedback effectiveness is measured at the listener's ear rather than solely by the speaker's intention.

## Decision rules

- If a manager wants to give difficult feedback, then first establish a credible pattern of soliciting and responding constructively to feedback, unless an immediate safety or misconduct issue requires prompt formal direction.
- If praise or criticism cannot identify a specific context, behavior, result, and expected next step, then refine it before delivery, unless the conversation is an initial inquiry rather than a conclusion.
- If the recipient becomes confused, sad, angry, or dismissive, then gauge the response and adjust care or clarity without abandoning the substantive issue.
- If the feedback concerns personality, identity, or vague attitude rather than observable behavior, then rewrite it around conduct and impact.
- If power, bias, discrimination, retaliation, or legal process makes open candor unsafe, then use protected reporting, documentation, or professional advice instead of insisting on direct interpersonal feedback.

## Action steps

1. Ask for criticism of your own behavior and demonstrate that you can receive it without punishment.
2. Prepare one specific observation, its meaningful result, and the requested next step.
3. Choose an appropriate channel: criticism privately and synchronously when practical; praise according to the recipient's preference and context.
4. Deliver the message humbly, clearly, promptly, and without making it about personality.
5. Pause and ask how the message landed; listen for misunderstanding, missing context, or emotional impact.
6. Agree on an action, support, owner, and review point.
7. Track patterns over time and use formal performance processes only with clear standards, evidence, support, and applicable safeguards.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Manager, private conversation soon after the event: "In yesterday's client review, the forecast changed but the risk was not flagged. The team committed to a date using incomplete information. What context am I missing? Going forward, please flag any forecast change in the shared tracker before the review."
- Manager soliciting upward feedback, private one-to-one: "What is one thing I do that makes your work harder? I will listen first, and I will tell you what I can change by Friday."
- Peer, private and timely: "When my point was interrupted twice in the planning meeting, the recommendation was left incomplete. Please let me finish the summary next time, and then challenge any part you disagree with."

## Counterexamples

- A manager who says harsh comments are radical candor but has not shown care, humility, specificity, or openness to feedback is practicing aggression, not the full framework.
- Immediate feedback can be harmful during acute distress, public embarrassment, or an unresolved safety incident.
- Public praise may embarrass a recipient or expose sensitive performance information.
- Inviting upward feedback without protecting dissent can increase retaliation risk.
- Direct feedback cannot repair unclear goals, discriminatory systems, impossible workload, or missing resources by itself.

## Contraindications

- Immediate danger, threats, violence, or severe psychological crisis
- Active harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or legal dispute where informal candor could prejudice safety or rights
- Formal investigations where contact with witnesses or subjects is restricted
- Use of candor as a justification for insults, coercion, public humiliation, or unsupported performance action

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Scott co-founded a company that sells training, coaching, workshops, and software based on the framework. Her examples are heavily influenced by senior leadership and United States technology companies, which can underrepresent lower-power workers, frontline settings, unions, public-sector constraints, and retaliation risk.
- Rule conflicts: ['The preference for prompt direct feedback can conflict with trauma-informed, legal, investigative, or union procedures.', 'Managerial informality can conflict with requirements for consistent written performance standards and due process.', "The framework's emphasis on personal care can blur boundaries if interpreted as requiring emotional intimacy.", 'Cultural norms may favor more indirect, private, or mediated challenge than the original examples imply.']
- Copyright scope: Safe use includes the public framework labels, short attributed phrases such as the two axes, bibliographic facts, and original paraphrases of feedback practices. Do not reproduce the publisher excerpt, diagrams, chapters, proprietary workbooks, assessments, or extended examples.
