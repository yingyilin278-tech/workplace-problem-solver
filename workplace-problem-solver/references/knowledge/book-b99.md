## Required user information

- The role, its outcomes, and the must-have versus nice-to-have skills.
- The hiring manager's authority, time, and decision criteria.
- The candidate's track record, references, and realistic job preview.
- The team's norms and any current biases in evaluation.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Hiring managers and interviewers.
- HR business partners and recruiters.
- Senior leaders approving headcount.

Power asymmetry:

Hiring decisions concentrate power. Apply structured evaluation, multiple perspectives, and bias checks to protect candidates and the team.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Anti-discrimination law, data protection, and right-to-work rules are jurisdiction-specific and must be observed.

## Core claims

- A good hiring process begins with a clear definition of the role's outcomes.
- Structured interviews and scorecards improve hiring accuracy.
- Realistic job previews reduce early attrition.
- Reference checks remain valuable when done with specificity.

## Decision rules

- If the must-have criteria are not met, the candidate is not a hire regardless of strength in nice-to-haves.
- If the interview feedback is dominated by one voice, run a structured debrief.
- If references are vague or absent, ask specific behavioral questions instead of generic impressions.
- If the candidate is being measured against an ideal that no current employee matches, recalibrate.

## Action steps

1. Write a one-page role description with must-have outcomes and skills.
2. Build a structured scorecard tied to those criteria.
3. Run the same set of behavioral questions for every candidate.
4. Debrief with the panel before discussing overall fit.
5. Conduct at least two reference conversations with specific questions.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: candidate; channel: interview; timing: at offer stage. Wording: "我们想确认三件事再发 offer：过去 [A 领域] 的可衡量成绩、[B 情况] 的处理方式，以及在 [C 团队] 的协作风格。"
- Audience: hiring panel; channel: debrief; timing: after interviews. Wording: "我们先各自按 scorecard 打分，再讨论；不在评分前讨论整体印象。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not substitute "culture fit" for a clear criteria conversation.
- Do not rely on a single interview or a single interviewer's impression.
- Do not skip reference checks for "obvious" hires.

## Contraindications

- Roles that require specific licenses or certifications.
- Hiring under anti-discrimination scrutiny; consult legal.
- Cases where the hiring decision is being driven by deadline pressure rather than evidence.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Practitioner book; specific methods vary by industry.
- Rule conflicts: ['Structured interviews reduce some biases but not all.', 'References are subject to legal limits on what can be asked.']
- Copyright scope: Summarize frameworks. Do not reproduce extended passages or scorecards.
