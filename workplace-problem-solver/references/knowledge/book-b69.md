## Required user information

- The specific people practice under review: hiring, evaluation, development, pay, or management.
- The business outcome the practice is intended to improve.
- The organization's size, maturity, industry, and legal jurisdiction.
- The current data quality, manager capability, and culture of feedback.
- The risk of importing large-company practices into small teams or low-resource settings.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- HR and people-operations leaders redesigning practices.
- Managers learning to give better feedback and run calibration.
- Senior leaders considering structural changes to performance, hiring, or rewards.

Power asymmetry:

The book's examples come from a high-resource, high-reputation organization. Many practices require manager time, analytical capability, and budget that smaller organizations may not have. Imported practices can also concentrate power if they are introduced without frontline input.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Several practices (hiring, evaluation, compensation transparency) are subject to local law. Direct transfer must be checked against labor regulations, data protection, and anti-discrimination requirements in the destination country.

## Core claims

- People practices should be designed around evidence, feedback, capability, and the employee experience rather than managerial intuition alone.
- Hiring, evaluation, development, and management are connected; improving one stage cannot compensate for a broken system elsewhere.
- Data can improve people decisions, but measurement can also distort behavior when indicators become targets.
- High-trust practices require clear standards, transparent expectations, and accountability—not permissiveness.

## Decision rules

- If a performance problem is alleged, distinguish capability, clarity, resources, behavior, and role fit before labeling the person.
- If a metric is used for appraisal, check whether it can be gamed and whether it captures the outcome that actually matters.
- If introducing a people-policy change, pilot it, define success measures, and collect employee feedback before scaling.
- If a company example conflicts with local law, contract, or organizational context, local requirements take priority.

## Action steps

1. Define the business or team outcome the people practice is meant to improve.
2. Identify the behavior or evidence that would indicate progress.
3. Check for confounders: manager differences, workload, role design, resources, and selection effects.
4. Run a bounded pilot with a comparison or before-and-after baseline where feasible.
5. Review both intended outcomes and side effects; revise the practice before formalizing it.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: manager; channel: one-on-one meeting; timing: when performance drops. Wording: "我们先把‘绩效不好’拆成目标是否清楚、资源是否够、能力是否匹配、行为是否达标四个问题，再决定支持、调整还是进入正式流程。"
- Audience: HR partner; channel: written brief; timing: when proposing a change. Wording: "这个指标只能作为参考，不能单独决定评价。我们同时看结果质量、过程证据和实际影响，避免为了指标牺牲真正目标。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not copy large-technology-company practices without checking scale, budget, culture, labor law, and power structure.
- Do not use employee surveys or performance data as covert surveillance.
- Do not turn a people-management framework into a legal conclusion or a promise of promotion.
- Do not use data to override qualitative signals of harm, bias, or unsafe conditions.

## Contraindications

- High-stakes individual decisions (termination, demotion) that require legal and procedural safeguards.
- Practices that require data the organization cannot collect or store lawfully.
- Situations where the underlying problem is unsafe management, discrimination, or unlawful conduct.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author was a senior Google people leader. The book is partly memoir of Google practice and partly advocacy; treat case studies as illustrative.
- Rule conflicts: ['Some practices (calibration, stack ranking) have been linked to bias and harm in research; adopt only with safeguards.', 'Aggressive data use can violate privacy and labor law in many jurisdictions.']
- Copyright scope: Use original paraphrases of the practice-design frame. Do not reproduce charts, internal Google documents, or extended passages; brief quotations only when necessary.
