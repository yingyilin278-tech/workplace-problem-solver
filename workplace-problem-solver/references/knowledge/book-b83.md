# B83 — The Body Keeps the Score

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation with clinical boundary
- Author: Bessel van der Kolk, psychiatrist and trauma researcher
- Source type: Clinical and research-based book on trauma
- Access scope: Official author and publisher materials plus structured secondary understanding for workplace boundary setting; not a diagnostic manual and not a replacement for licensed clinical evaluation
- Evidence tier: Widely cited trauma-focused clinical and research synthesis with workplace relevance mainly through trigger recognition, safety, accommodation, and referral boundaries
- Coverage: C5, C10, C11

Official sources:

- https://www.besselvanderkolk.com/resources/the-body-keeps-the-score
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/165616/the-body-keeps-the-score-by-bessel-van-der-kolk-md/9780143127741/

## Required user information

- The user's symptoms and the contexts that trigger them.
- Whether a qualified mental-health professional is involved.
- The workplace demands, accommodations available, and legal protections.
- The user's history of trauma, treatment, and current support.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- People dealing with the workplace impact of trauma.
- Managers and HR partners supporting accommodations.
- Coaches and EAP providers within their scope of practice.

Power asymmetry:

The book is not a self-diagnosis guide. Workplace applications must remain within the user's access to professional support and the organization's accommodation policy.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Mental-health accommodations, leave, and disability rights are jurisdiction-specific. Confirm the applicable framework in the user's location.

## Core claims

- Trauma can affect body, mind, and behavior long after the event.
- Traditional talk therapy is one of several effective approaches; body-based and relational approaches also matter.
- Safe relationships, predictable routines, and agency are central to recovery.
- Workplace triggers can re-enact trauma; reasonable accommodations can reduce harm.

## Decision rules

- If the user is in acute distress, prioritize safety and professional help over performance.
- If the workplace is part of the problem, identify the specific triggers and possible accommodations.
- If the user is in treatment, coordinate with their provider on what the workplace can change.
- If the manager is the source of harm, do not ask the user to manage the manager alone.

## Action steps

1. Document specific triggers, time patterns, and physical reactions.
2. Identify the smallest workplace changes that reduce triggers.
3. Request formal accommodations through HR or a medical note.
4. Build a recovery routine outside work hours.
5. Review the accommodations and the treatment plan with a professional.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: self or therapist; channel: private note; timing: when symptoms flare. Wording: "我今天被 [事件] 触发了，身体反应是 [描述]，我会先离开会议室、走到安全的地方，再继续讨论。"
- Audience: HR; channel: written request; timing: at the start of accommodation. Wording: "医生建议我在 [具体情形] 中减少 [具体任务/环境]，请问公司可以提供的调整有哪些？"

## Counterexamples

- Do not self-diagnose serious trauma.
- Do not pressure disclosure of trauma details to a manager.
- Do not use the framework to justify abusive behavior toward colleagues.

## Contraindications

- Active suicidal ideation or self-harm risk—escalate to emergency services.
- Acute psychiatric crisis requiring inpatient care.
- Workplace situations that are themselves traumatic and ongoing.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is a senior clinician and researcher; framework is widely cited but clinical practice should be supervised.
- Rule conflicts: ['Some workplace demands may not be accommodable without role change.', 'Self-application has limits; professional support is required for serious trauma.']
- Copyright scope: Summarize concepts. Do not reproduce clinical case material.
