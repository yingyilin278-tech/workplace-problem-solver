# B77 — Range

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: David Epstein, author and journalist writing on performance, learning, and career development
- Source type: Research-based trade book drawing on multiple disciplines
- Coverage: C8, C11, C12

Official sources:

- https://davidepstein.com/books/range/
- https://www.macmillan.com/books/9781250110880/range

## Required user information

- Whether the user is facing a narrow specialist path, a broad exploratory path, or a forced change.
- The user's prior experience, transferable skills, and learning preferences.
- The urgency, reversibility, and downside of the current decision.
- Any constraints from employer, industry, licensing, or family.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Early-career professionals choosing between specialization and exploration.
- Mid-career changers considering transfer to a new function.
- Managers and parents supporting learning pathways.

Power asymmetry:

Encouragement of "range" is not a guarantee of equal opportunity. People with financial flexibility, visa stability, and employer support have more room to experiment than those without.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Concepts transfer across contexts, but licensing, credentialing, and labor-market rules are jurisdiction-specific.

## Core claims

- In complex, novel domains, broad experience and analogical thinking often outperform narrow specialization.
- In rule-based, well-defined domains, deep specialization has clear advantages.
- "Late bloomers" frequently outperform early specialists in fields requiring pattern recognition across experiences.
- Sampling multiple paths early is not wasteful when the long-term match is uncertain.

## Decision rules

- If the user is in a rule-based, high-stakes domain (e.g., surgery, accounting), protect deep practice time.
- If the user is in a complex, ambiguous domain, value cross-domain exposure.
- If the user is facing a forced change, inventory transferable skills before assuming a "restart."
- If the user is locked into a narrow path by employer, family, or financial pressure, design low-cost experiments rather than abrupt jumps.

## Action steps

1. List the skills the user has used across jobs, projects, and hobbies.
2. Identify which skills are domain-specific and which are transferable.
3. Test the match through a bounded project, course, or volunteer commitment.
4. Document evidence of learning and impact to support a future transition.
5. Build a small portfolio of cross-domain work samples.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: career changer; channel: one-on-one; timing: planning. Wording: "在动手前先做一个小项目验证匹配度，两到四周足够看出节奏和难度是否合适。"
- Audience: manager; channel: written note; timing: assigning work. Wording: "这个任务你可能没做过，但里面有三项可迁移技能，先用这两周时间验证，再决定是否扩到主线。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not romanticize exploration when the user has urgent financial or family obligations.
- Do not discourage necessary specialization in safety-critical fields.
- Do not assume that a wide range of experiences automatically leads to mastery.

## Contraindications

- Required certifications or licenses for the target role.
- High-debt, high-dependency situations where a risky transition is unsafe.
- Roles where regulatory or safety requirements demand a single specialized path.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is a journalist; framework is broadly applicable but the empirical evidence is mixed.
- Rule conflicts: ['Specialization is essential in some regulated domains; the model does not override licensing needs.', 'Breadth can delay income in the short term.']
- Copyright scope: Use original paraphrases. Do not quote extended case passages or figures.
