## Required user information

- The team's current strategy, mission, and annual priorities.
- The existing measurement system and how it is used in decisions.
- The team's appetite for stretch goals and review cadence.
- Cultural factors that affect transparency and accountability.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Senior leaders setting strategy.
- Managers running quarterly and annual reviews.
- Teams adopting or refining OKRs.

Power asymmetry:

OKRs work best with psychological safety; if performance management is punitive, OKRs can amplify blame.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

OKRs are widely transferable; specific compensation, evaluation, and labor rules remain jurisdiction-specific.

## Core claims

- Objectives and Key Results (OKRs) align teams around measurable outcomes and ambitious goals.
- Good OKRs are public, time-bound, and stretch but not punishing.
- The scoring system (0.0 to 1.0) encourages honest self-assessment.
- OKRs enable focus, alignment, and tracking of stretch work without micromanagement.

## Decision rules

- If the team is doing too many things, the OKR is the place to cut, not add.
- If the score is consistently 1.0, the objectives are not stretch enough.
- If the score is consistently 0.3, the objectives or resources are misaligned.
- If the team is gaming the metric, redesign the objective, not just the score.

## Action steps

1. Set one to three quarterly objectives with three to five key results each.
2. Make OKRs visible to the team and to peer groups.
3. Review weekly or biweekly with a focus on leading indicators.
4. Score honestly at quarter end; share the score and the learning.
5. Re-evaluate the next quarter's OKRs based on prior learning.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: team; channel: meeting; timing: at quarter start. Wording: "这个季度我们的目标是什么？哪三个结果能证明我们做到了？"
- Audience: leadership; channel: written note; timing: at quarter end. Wording: "我们的最终得分是 [0-1]；哪个 KR 偏离最大，我们学到了什么？"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use OKRs to set personal performance targets tied to compensation in a punitive way.
- Do not grade the score as a school test; the value is in learning.
- Do not require more than three objectives per cycle; focus beats coverage.

## Contraindications

- Cultures where transparency is unsafe or where scores are weaponized.
- Roles that require precise, regulated metrics not suited to OKR-style stretch goals.
