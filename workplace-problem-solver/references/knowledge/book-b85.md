# B85 — Dare to Lead

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Brené Brown, research professor and author writing on leadership and courage
- Source type: Research-based book on values-driven leadership
- Coverage: C2, C3, C7, C8, C10, C12

Official sources:

- https://brenebrown.com/books/dare-to-lead/
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/564063/dare-to-lead-by-brene-brown-phd/9780399592522/

## Required user information

- The user's leadership stage and the team they lead.
- The values they are willing to defend versus compromise.
- Recent courageous conversations and how they went.
- The organizational culture's tolerance for vulnerability.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Managers and senior leaders.
- Team leads running values-driven teams.
- Educators and community organizers.

Power asymmetry:

The framework assumes a degree of psychological safety. Apply it cautiously where retaliation is likely; the first priority is safety, not vulnerability.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The model transfers to many leadership contexts but must be adapted to local norms on disclosure and authority.

## Core claims

- Courageous leadership requires four skill sets: rumbling with vulnerability, living values, BRAVING trust, and learning to rise.
- Most leadership failures involve avoiding hard conversations rather than lacking skills.
- Clear values and clear boundaries enable teams to take risks.
- Trust is built through specific behaviors, not through general goodwill.

## Decision rules

- If the user is avoiding a hard conversation, identify the fear driving the avoidance.
- If the team's values are not explicit, write them down and use them in decisions.
- If trust is low, name the specific behaviors that would rebuild it.
- If a leader's values conflict with organizational norms, decide whether to stay and lead change, or leave.

## Action steps

1. Write down the user's three core leadership values and three non-negotiables.
2. List the conversations the user has been avoiding in the last 90 days.
3. Pick one and schedule it within the next two weeks.
4. Use a feedback framework that separates behavior, impact, and request.
5. Review the outcome and document learning.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: team; channel: meeting; timing: when setting expectations. Wording: "我们对 [具体行为] 的标准是 [标准]，因为这关系到 [价值]。偏离这个标准时我们会直接说出来。"
- Audience: direct report; channel: one-on-one; timing: when giving feedback. Wording: "我说的不是你的能力，而是这次具体的行为，因为这是我们共同的标准。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use vulnerability as a strategy to avoid accountability.
- Do not use values language to override legal obligations or safety rules.
- Do not pressure people to share personal stories in unsafe contexts.

## Contraindications

- Active retaliation, harassment, or discrimination.
- Workplaces that lack basic procedural safeguards.
- Situations requiring formal investigation, not values-based conversation.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is a researcher and public speaker; framework is widely adopted.
- Rule conflicts: ['Values-based language can be misused to override HR or legal requirements.', 'Trust-building assumes reciprocity; in asymmetric power, slower progress is realistic.']
- Copyright scope: Summarize the four skill sets. Do not reproduce extended passages or worksheets.
