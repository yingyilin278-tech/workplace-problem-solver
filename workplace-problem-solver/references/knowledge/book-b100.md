## Required user information

- The user's current role, energy patterns, and dissatisfaction points.
- The user's longer-term life and work goals.
- Constraints: finances, caregiving, geography, immigration status.
- The user's willingness to prototype before committing.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Individuals seeking to redesign their work.
- Coaches and career advisors.
- People navigating mid-career transitions.

Power asymmetry:

Some users face structural constraints (visa, caregiving) that limit prototypes; adapt the framework to those constraints.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The methods are broadly applicable; specific contract, immigration, and licensing rules are jurisdiction-specific.

## Core claims

- Work is a set of problems to be designed, not a fixed path.
- Energy, not just time, is the key resource to design around.
- Prototyping—short, low-cost experiments—reduces the risk of major changes.
- Reframing problems often reveals more actionable options.

## Decision rules

- If the user is stuck, generate at least three new framings of the problem before deciding.
- If the user is burned out, identify the energy drains and the energy sources before changing role.
- If a change is significant, run a prototype for two to four weeks before committing.
- If the user has multiple interests, design for a portfolio, not a single calling.

## Action steps

1. Map the user's current week's energy: where does it rise, where does it drop?
2. List the worst work problems and reframe each in three ways.
3. Identify three prototypes that test new options in two to four weeks.
4. Schedule a weekly reflection on the prototypes.
5. Decide to keep, adjust, or abandon each prototype.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: self or coach; channel: private note; timing: weekly. Wording: "本周我能量最高和最低的时刻分别是什么？我接下来能怎么设计？"
- Audience: peer; channel: conversation; timing: when comparing paths. Wording: "我可以做两周的小项目验证这个方向，再决定要不要换。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not treat "follow your passion" as the only path.
- Do not prototype at the expense of existing commitments without planning.
- Do not assume energy patterns are fixed; they shift with context.

## Contraindications

- Cases requiring immediate income and unable to prototype safely.
- Users in acute mental-health crisis where change is destabilizing.
- Roles where the user is in a legally protected leave or accommodation window.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Authors are design program leaders; the framework is design-oriented and assumes some choice.
