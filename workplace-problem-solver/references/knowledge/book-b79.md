## Required user information

- The manager's current level: tech lead, new manager, senior manager, or director.
- Team size, stage, and degree of technical complexity.
- Whether the user is managing individual contributors, other managers, or both.
- Recent feedback, performance cycles, and team health signals.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- New and experienced managers of technical or knowledge work.
- Leaders transitioning from individual contributor to people manager.
- HR business partners supporting manager development.

Power asymmetry:

Power differentials in management make candor difficult. Encourage documented feedback, skip-level conversations, and clear escalation paths.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Manager practices are subject to local labor law on hiring, evaluation, and termination. Adapt the book to local compliance requirements.

## Core claims

- Management is a series of transitions, each with new skills and new traps.
- New managers often fail by acting as super-individual-contributors rather than as enablers.
- Senior managers must shift from direct mentoring to system design and talent selection.
- Career conversations and feedback should be ongoing, not reserved for performance reviews.

## Decision rules

- If a new manager is doing all the hardest work themselves, redirect them to delegation and feedback.
- If a senior manager is still making most decisions, redesign the system for delegation and review.
- If a team is unclear about priorities, the manager's first job is to clarify and defend priorities.
- If a difficult conversation has been delayed, do not let performance review be the first time it surfaces.

## Action steps

1. Identify the current transition stage and the skills it requires.
2. Schedule regular one-on-ones with direct reports and skip-levels.
3. Document priorities, expectations, and success criteria for the team.
4. Practice giving and receiving feedback in low-stakes settings before high-stakes ones.
5. Review career growth conversations at least quarterly.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: direct report; channel: one-on-one; timing: mid-cycle. Wording: "我注意到你最近的工作节奏与上次目标不完全一致，可以先讲讲你看到的情况吗？我们一起把目标和资源对齐。"
- Audience: peer manager; channel: written note; timing: when handing off a project. Wording: "从今天起我不在这个项目上直接做决定，由你决定排期和取舍，需要时再找我。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use the framework to avoid holding poor performers accountable.
- Do not treat all manager mistakes as developmental edges that will resolve themselves.
- Do not encourage managers to bypass HR or legal review for sensitive cases.

## Contraindications

- Active harassment, discrimination, or retaliation claims.
- Termination decisions that require formal procedure.
- Cases where the manager's own performance is the issue.
