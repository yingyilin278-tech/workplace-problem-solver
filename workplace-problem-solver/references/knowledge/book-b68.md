## Required user information

- The person's current task design, level of autonomy, skill growth, and feedback.
- Whether the role already offers adequate pay, safety, and basic fairness.
- The specific outcome the user wants: promotion, learning, recognition, control, or stability.
- The risk of framing motivation advice as a substitute for adequate pay or safe conditions.
- The user's industry, organization, and country, since autonomy and mastery are not equally available in every context.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Individual contributors considering scope changes.
- Managers designing roles, recognition, and team practices.
- HR and people-operations partners redesigning engagement programs.

Power asymmetry:

Autonomy, mastery, and purpose cannot compensate for inadequate compensation, unsafe conditions, or discrimination. If the underlying conditions are broken, motivation framing alone is misleading and can be used to deflect structural problems.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The book's research is based mainly on Western, English-language, knowledge-economy samples. Direct transfer to low-wage, high-surveillance, or collectivist workplaces requires caution.

## Core claims

- Durable motivation is not reducible to external rewards; autonomy, mastery, and purpose are useful diagnostic lenses for work design.
- Rewards can narrow attention or weaken intrinsic engagement when they are controlling, unpredictable, or attached to work that people already value.
- Autonomy means meaningful discretion within clear constraints, not absence of accountability.
- Mastery is ongoing improvement toward a valued capability, not a promise of immediate promotion.
- Purpose connects daily work to a meaningful outcome, but purpose language cannot compensate for unfair pay, unsafe conditions, or abusive management.

## Decision rules

- If a team shows low engagement, first check control, skill growth, feedback, and meaning before adding bonuses.
- If a person asks for promotion, separate title/status from the underlying need for scope, learning, recognition, or compensation.
- If discretion increases risk or quality problems, preserve non-negotiable standards and offer autonomy over method, sequencing, or execution.
- If motivation advice is used to avoid pay or staffing problems, reject the framing and address the structural issue.

## Action steps

1. Ask what part of the work the person wants more control over, what capability they want to build, and what outcome matters to them.
2. Define a small area of decision authority with explicit boundaries and review points.
3. Agree on a capability goal and observable evidence of progress.
4. Connect the task to its actual user, customer, or organizational outcome without exaggerating its importance.
5. Review whether the redesign improved behavior, quality, and wellbeing rather than only short-term output.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: employee; channel: one-on-one meeting; timing: after a small task. Wording: "你希望在这项工作里增加哪一部分自主权？我们把可自主决定的范围、必须遵守的标准和复盘时间先写清楚。"
- Audience: manager; channel: written note; timing: after a promotion discussion. Wording: "这次不先承诺晋升，但可以先明确你要承担的范围、需要补齐的能力，以及达到什么证据后再谈岗位变化。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use "purpose" to justify unpaid work or unreasonable overtime.
- Do not assume every worker values autonomy over stability, pay, status, or workload reduction.
- Do not remove management support under the banner of autonomy.
- Do not apply the model to short-tenure, safety-critical, or newly trained roles where standardized practice is required.

## Contraindications

- Safety-critical, regulated, or newly trained work where autonomy must be temporarily limited.
- Roles where the worker is being pressured to accept unsafe or exploitative conditions through "purpose" language.
- Performance management that uses mastery language to avoid pay or staffing issues.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Trade-book author and speaker; framework is widely cited but the operational thresholds (how much autonomy, what kind of feedback) come from secondary literature.
- Rule conflicts: ['Some performance systems require tightly prescribed behavior; autonomy must be calibrated to risk.', 'In collectivist or hierarchical settings, the framing of "individual autonomy" may not match team expectations.']
- Copyright scope: Use original paraphrases of the autonomy-mastery-purpose frame. Do not reproduce extended passages or case studies; brief quotations only when necessary.
