# B86 — Leaders Eat Last

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Simon Sinek, author and speaker on leadership and organizational trust
- Source type: Practitioner book with illustrative cases
- Coverage: C2, C5, C8, C10, C12

Official sources:

- https://simonsinek.com/books/leaders-eat-last/
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/308927/leaders-eat-last-by-simon-sinek/9781591848011/

## Required user information

- The current state of trust between the user and the team.
- Whether the user is currently in a position to influence team conditions.
- The team's stressors and recent changes in workload or direction.
- The organizational values the user is willing to model.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Managers and senior leaders.
- Team leads seeking to model values.
- HR and culture partners.

Power asymmetry:

Leadership is the dominant factor in team culture. Use the model as a leader or advocate; do not ask individual contributors to do the leader's job.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The book's examples come from U.S. business and military contexts. Adapt to local norms, regulations, and labor law.

## Core claims

- Trust and safety are the foundation of effective teams.
- Leaders who model sacrifice and empathy build cultures of voluntary cooperation.
- The biological effects of chronic workplace stress undermine performance and health.
- Circles of safety enable innovation and resilience.

## Decision rules

- If the user is a leader and has not asked the team about their biggest obstacle, do that before changing strategy.
- If the team's stress signals are visible, treat them as urgent.
- If a "leader" consistently puts self-interest first, the team's trust will erode predictably.
- If the user is not in a leadership role, focus on what they can control: how they treat colleagues and what they escalate.

## Action steps

1. Identify the team's biggest source of stress this week.
2. Remove one obstacle the leader can actually remove.
3. Publicly acknowledge the team's effort and the cost of recent changes.
4. Ask the team what would make their work more sustainable.
5. Document the changes made and review in 30 days.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: team; channel: meeting; timing: at the start of a new project. Wording: "这个项目会很紧张，我的工作是先帮你们减少障碍，不是再加码。如果我说到没做到，直接告诉我。"
- Audience: leader; channel: private note; timing: weekly review. Wording: "我今天帮团队做了什么或没做什么？这是我应该承担的责任。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use empathy language to avoid hard decisions.
- Do not use the model to justify protecting underperformers at the team's expense.
- Do not promise safety the organization cannot deliver.

## Contraindications

- Active safety, compliance, or legal risk that requires formal handling.
- Cultures where "trust" rhetoric is used to cover unsafe practices.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is a public speaker; case examples are illustrative.
- Rule conflicts: ['Trust-building cannot override mandatory legal or compliance duties.', 'Some organizational stressors require structural change, not just leadership modeling.']
