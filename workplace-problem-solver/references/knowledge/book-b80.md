## Required user information

- The user's prior experience as an individual contributor.
- The size and stage of the new team.
- Whether the user has peers who have been managers before.
- Existing performance management and feedback infrastructure in the organization.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- First-time managers.
- Recently promoted team leads.
- HR partners supporting manager onboarding.

Power asymmetry:

New managers often inherit former peers. Power dynamics must be handled with explicit agreements, role clarification, and a clean transition announcement.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Manager practices are subject to local labor law on hiring, evaluation, and termination. Adapt the framework to local compliance requirements.

## Core claims

- Management is a learnable craft, not an innate trait.
- Most first-time failures are predictable: avoiding hard conversations, not delegating, trying to do everything, and over-identifying with individual contributors.
- Simple rituals (regular one-on-ones, written priorities, weekly notes) outperform complex frameworks in the first year.
- Asking good questions is more effective than giving good answers.

## Decision rules

- If a new manager is reluctant to give critical feedback, treat avoidance as the primary development area.
- If a former peer is now a report, run an explicit conversation about the change in relationship.
- If the team is unclear on priorities, set three or fewer quarterly priorities and protect them.
- If a manager is working more hours than the team, the delegation system is broken.

## Action steps

1. Set up weekly 30-minute one-on-ones with every direct report.
2. Write down the team's top three quarterly priorities and review monthly.
3. Identify one decision authority to delegate and review the outcome.
4. Practice one difficult feedback conversation per month with coaching support.
5. Document decisions and share notes with the team.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: direct report (former peer); channel: one-on-one; timing: in the first month. Wording: "我们现在的工作关系和以前不一样了，我们把新的合作方式、决策范围和反馈机制明确一下。"
- Audience: skip-level; channel: written or verbal; timing: monthly. Wording: "我希望每月有一次和你直接沟通的机会，不一定是出问题时才谈。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not treat the framework as a substitute for holding poor performers accountable.
- Do not encourage managers to be everyone's friend.
- Do not rely on a single "tough conversation" to fix a long-standing problem.

## Contraindications

- Harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or safety issues that require formal procedure.
- Termination decisions that require legal and procedural safeguards.
- Cases where the manager is the source of harm.
