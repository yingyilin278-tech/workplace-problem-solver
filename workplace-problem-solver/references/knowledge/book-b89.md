# B89 — The Power of Others

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Michael Bond, author and writer on behavioral science
- Source type: Research-based book on social influence and well-being
- Coverage: C5, C8, C10, C12

Official sources:

- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/566238/the-power-of-others-by-michael-bond/9781615193833/

## Required user information

- The user's current social support and the stressors they face.
- The quality of the user's close relationships at work and outside.
- The user's openness to feedback and the safety of their environment.
- The user's goals for change.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Individuals navigating change or stress.
- Managers supporting team members.
- Coaches and mentors within their scope of practice.

Power asymmetry:

Social influence can be used to manipulate; use the framework to support autonomy, not to pressure or coerce.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

Cultural norms around privacy, family involvement, and disclosure vary. Adapt suggestions to the user's context.

## Core claims

- People are profoundly shaped by the social environments they move through.
- "Peer shocks"—the people who surround us at key transitions—have outsized effects on our behavior, health, and decisions.
- Choosing a better peer group is one of the highest-leverage personal development moves.
- Strong, supportive relationships are linked to measurable health and performance benefits.

## Decision rules

- If the user is stuck in a toxic environment, treat escape as a valid development move.
- If the user's peer group reinforces harmful behavior, identify who in the user's life models a better path.
- If the user is a manager, recognize that they set the tone of the team's peer environment.
- If the user is isolated, build a small, intentional peer group before taking on major changes.

## Action steps

1. Map the user's current peer group and the behaviors each person models.
2. Identify one or two people whose habits the user wants to learn.
3. Increase exposure to those people through projects, communities, or mentorship.
4. Reduce exposure to peers whose norms conflict with the user's goals.
5. Review the user's trajectory after 90 days.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: self or coach; channel: private note; timing: at a transition. Wording: "我现在的 5 个最常接触的人，是否符合我想成为的方向？"
- Audience: manager; channel: meeting; timing: when assigning a peer. Wording: "这次合作是有意安排的，让 [A] 和 [B] 在一起，是因为他们各自的强项可以互补。"

## Counterexamples

- Do not pressure the user to cut off all existing relationships.
- Do not assume one peer replacement is enough for deep change.
- Do not use the framework to justify group pressure or exclusion.

## Contraindications

- Cases where the user's environment is itself dangerous and requires immediate safety planning.
- Therapeutic relationships that require professional referral.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is a science writer; framework is based on multiple social-influence studies.
- Rule conflicts: ['Some peer effects are structural, not chosen.', 'Not every transition is best handled by changing peers.']
- Copyright scope: Summarize the peer-shock concept. Do not reproduce extended passages or figures.
