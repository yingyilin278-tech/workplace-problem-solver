# B91 — Give and Take

## Source status

- Enablement level: L2, pending case validation
- Author: Adam Grant, organizational psychologist and professor at Wharton
- Source type: Research-based book on reciprocity and networks
- Coverage: C4, C6, C8, C12

Official sources:

- https://adamgrant.net/book/give-and-take/
- https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/176762/give-and-take-by-adam-grant/9780143124986/

## Required user information

- The user's current reciprocity style: giver, matcher, or taker.
- The user's network composition and norms.
- The user's workload and the cost of helping.
- The user's current goals and time horizon.

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Individual contributors and managers.
- Sales, service, and consulting professionals.
- HR and L&D partners designing reciprocity norms.

Power asymmetry:

Givers can be exploited in cutthroat environments. Combine the framework with safeguards against overwork, discrimination, and harassment.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The book's evidence is largely from U.S. knowledge work. Adapt to local norms on networking and helping.

## Core claims

- Givers, takers, and matchers perform differently depending on context and self-awareness.
- Otherish givers—those who help others while protecting their own interests—often outperform pure givers and takers.
- Five-minute favors and weak-tie networks are high-leverage ways to build reciprocity.
- Helping others can be a sustainable path to influence, not a path to burnout.

## Decision rules

- If the user is a pure giver burning out, set a "giver budget" and learn to say no.
- If the user is in a taker-heavy environment, identify matchers and otherish givers before offering help.
- If the user wants to expand influence, offer specific, low-cost help to a wider circle.
- If the user's network is too narrow, invest in weak ties outside the immediate team.

## Action steps

1. Identify the user's default reciprocity style and its costs.
2. Set a weekly giving budget in time or task count.
3. Identify one weak tie to reconnect with this month.
4. Decline one request that does not fit the budget.
5. Review the user's network and reputation quarterly.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: peer; channel: message; timing: when offering help. Wording: "这次我可以帮你 30 分钟；如果需要更多，我们另外约时间。"
- Audience: manager; channel: meeting; timing: when delegating. Wording: "我看到 [同事] 在 [领域] 很忙，我可以介绍 [另一人] 帮 30 分钟，你看好不好？"

## Counterexamples

- Do not use givers as the default for unpaid extra work.
- Do not assume giving is always high-value; protect givers from exploitation.
- Do not use the framework to pressure people into uncompensated help.

## Contraindications

- Workplaces that systematically exploit givers.
- Situations where "help" crosses into sexual, financial, or other misconduct boundaries.

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Author is an organizational psychologist; framework draws on U.S. studies.
- Rule conflicts: ['Pure givers can be exploited; safeguards are necessary.', 'Reciprocity norms vary across cultures and industries.']
