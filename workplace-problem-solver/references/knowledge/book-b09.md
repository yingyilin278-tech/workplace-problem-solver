## Required user information

- The observable event without labels or motive claims
- The user's feelings and the underlying work need or value
- The specific, feasible action requested from the other person
- Whether the other person can genuinely refuse
- Power, safety, retaliation, and formal-duty constraints

## Applicable roles, power, and culture

Applicable roles:

- Employees
- Managers
- Facilitators and mediators
- Teams seeking lower-escalation communication

Power asymmetry:

A language of requests can reduce blame, but a request is not freely negotiable when one party controls pay, ratings, continued employment, or safety. Managers must not relabel commands as requests, and employees should not be expected to empathize their way out of coercion.

Jurisdiction and cultural transfer:

The model is used internationally, but naming feelings and needs directly can feel unusually intimate in some workplace cultures. Adapt the vocabulary to professional norms while preserving observation, impact, request, and choice. Legal duties and organizational procedures remain controlling.

## Core claims

- Author-scoped claim: moralistic judgments, labels, comparisons, denial of responsibility, and demands can increase defensiveness or block compassion.
- Author-scoped claim: directly expressing needs and values is preferable to implying that another person is wrong or bad.
- Author-scoped claim: communication should distinguish requests from demands and acknowledge personal choice and responsibility.
- Publisher-scoped claim: the NVC process integrates consciousness, language, communication, and influence based on power with rather than power over.

## Decision rules

- If a draft message contains a character label or motive accusation, replace it with a concrete observation before sending.
- If the speaker wants behavior change, name the work need and make a specific request that can be answered.
- If refusal would trigger blame or punishment, treat the message as a demand and clarify the actual authority and consequences.
- Unless rights, safety, or formal accountability require stronger action, listen for the other party's needs before proposing a joint solution.
- If a manager must issue a lawful directive, state the directive and reason honestly rather than disguising it as optional.

## Action steps

1. Pause and identify judgments, comparisons, and assumptions in the current story.
2. Write the observable behavior in neutral, time-bounded terms.
3. Name the feeling or work impact without assigning emotional causation to the other person.
4. Identify the need, value, or work requirement at stake.
5. Make one concrete, feasible request and listen to the response.
6. If agreement is not possible, move to boundaries, authority, policy, mediation, or escalation.

## Ready-to-use wording

- Audience: Coworker
- Channel and timing: Private conversation; Soon after a specific repeated behavior

> In the last two handoffs, the file arrived after the agreed deadline, and I could not complete the client review on time. I need a reliable handoff point. Would you send the final file by 3 p.m. tomorrow or tell me by noon if that is not possible?

- Audience: Direct report
- Channel and timing: One-to-one meeting; During corrective feedback

> The last three reports omitted the risk section required by the template. I am concerned because the review team cannot assess exposure. Please use the current template for Friday's report, and tell me what support you need to do that.

## Counterexamples

- A feelings-and-needs formula can sound artificial, patronizing, or emotionally invasive in a concise professional setting.
- Empathy does not replace accountability for fraud, harassment, safety violations, or repeated nonperformance.
- A person with formal power can misuse NVC language to soften coercion while leaving the threat unchanged.
- Neutral observation becomes misleading when it omits a documented pattern or material consequence.

## Contraindications

- Immediate danger, threats, or violence
- Situations requiring formal reporting, investigation, or preservation of evidence
- Legal disputes where a communication model cannot determine rights or remedies
- Interactions where disclosure of feelings or needs would create a foreseeable safety or retaliation risk

## Conflicts and limitations

- Author bias and conflicts: Rosenberg founded the movement associated with the model, and the publisher and training ecosystem sell related books and programs. The framework has a normative commitment to compassion and collaborative power, which may underweight adversarial incentives or formal accountability.
- Rule conflicts: NVC's emphasis on nonjudgment and shared needs can conflict with duties to name discrimination, misconduct, fraud, or unsafe behavior clearly. Employment law, trauma-informed safety, formal authority, and evidence-preservation requirements may require more direct or procedural action.
- Copyright scope: Paraphrase the observation-feeling-need-request logic and the distinction between requests and demands. Do not reproduce the chapter sample, extended examples, exercises, or distinctive passages. Keep any necessary quotation brief and attributed.
